  16. Two goals

The planning lead read the two plans of the lesson Value: NPV by start year and risk reduction and asked for something in between:

The NPV plan leaves too much risk on the table, and the risk plan gives up $5.7M of NPV. Show us the options in between: for each extra bit of risk removed, how much NPV does it cost? And let us set a floor on risk removed and get the best NPV above it.

One Objective Gives One End of the Trade

steps/04 maximizes one measure, chosen by the request's objective setting. Its two plans are the ends of the trade: the most NPV ($24,260k, removing $3,709k of risk) and the most risk removed ($6,828k, worth $18,559k). The code has no way to ask for a plan between them, because the request has nowhere to put a floor and the model has no row for one. It also has a weaker flaw at each end: when two plans tie on the measure that is maximized, the solver returns either, and one of them may remove less risk for the same NPV.

A Floor on Risk Turns the Trade Into a Series of NPV Solves

Section 8 offered three ways to trade two goals:

MethodForAgainst
weighted sum, $\max\ \text{NPV} + w \cdot \text{risk}$ (lesson Weighted sums and what the weights really mean)one solve per weightthe weight has no unit a planner can set; it finds only supported points (lesson Supported and unsupported Pareto points)
lexicographic, one goal then the other within a tolerance (lesson Hierarchical objectives with tolerances)settles ties at each endgives one plan, not the trade
ε-constraint, $\max\ \text{NPV}$ with $\text{risk} \ge \varepsilon$ for a series of $\varepsilon$ (lesson The ε-constraint method), each point finished lexicographically (chosen)the floor is in $k, a number planners can read and set; finds unsupported points tootwo solves per point

The planner uses ε-constraint for the frontier and for the planners' floor, and the lexicographic second solve for every point, so no point is beaten on one goal by a plan equal on the other (the weakly efficient points of lesson AUGMECON: avoiding weakly efficient points).

The Model

The model of the lesson Value: NPV by start year and risk reduction, with a risk floor row and the two-solve recipe of each point.

Sets. $P$, $Y$, $T_i$, $M$, $\mathcal{G}$, $\mathcal{B}$, $C$ as in the lesson Business rules: must-dos, bundles, parents and alternatives.

Parameters. $c_{i,k}$, $B_y$, $\text{npv}_{i,t}$, $r_{i,t}$, $L_c$ as in the lesson Value: NPV by start year and risk reduction; $\varepsilon$, the floor on risk removed ($k), from the planners or from the frontier's grid; $\text{NPV}^*$, the NPV the first solve found ($k).

Variables. $x_{i,t} \in \{0, 1\}$ for every $i \in P$, $t \in T_i$.

Write $\text{NPV}(x) = \sum_{i,t} \text{npv}_{i,t}\, x_{i,t}$ and $\text{R}(x) = \sum_{i,t} r_{i,t}\, x_{i,t}$.

Constraints.

NPVs are whole $k, so two plans with different NPV differ by at least 1, and a slack of 0.5 keeps the first solve's NPV without depending on the solver's last digit. For the risk end the roles swap: maximize $\text{R}$, then $\text{NPV}$ with $\text{R}(x) \ge \text{R}^* - 10^{-6}$ (keep_risk), a tiny slack because risk is a float sum.

cipt/goals.py. Before: the stub of the lesson Value: NPV by start year and risk reduction, frontier returning an empty list, with the record Point already in place. After, the file starts with the two slacks and that record. From steps/05/cipt/goals.py:

KEEP holds the slack of keep_npv and keep_risk, keyed by the goal the second solve keeps. A Point is one plan of the frontier with its two values, its NPV and the risk it removes, in $k. Two functions of steps/05/cipt/goals.py do the work. best(request, first, floor=None) is the two-solve recipe of one point, and the page leaves its code to the practice. first names the goal of the first solve, "npv" or "risk"; the other goal is the one first does not name.

  1. Build the model once. When a floor is given, add the row $\text{risk\_floor}$ of the model above, $\text{R}(x) \ge \varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon$ the floor, under the name risk_floor.
  2. Maximize the first goal, solve (1) of the model above, with solve_built. A plan whose status is not "optimal" is returned as it is.
  3. Otherwise add the keep row: $\text{keep\_npv}$ when first is "npv", which holds the goal at least at the first solve's value minus its slack in KEEP, under the name keep_<first>. For first set to "risk" it is keep_risk, the same row on $\text{R}$ with the risk slack. The first solve's value is the plan's npv or risk.
  4. Maximize the other goal, solve (2), and return that plan.

The second solve reuses the same Built: one row added, the objective replaced. frontier calls best for each end and each floor, in this order: the NPV end, the floors from low to high, the risk end. From steps/05/cipt/goals.py:

Then it drops points in two passes, repeats first. A plan is kept when it is optimal and its NPV is not within 0.5 of a point already kept; of two plans with the same NPV, the earlier in the order above stays. Only then are dominated points dropped, among the points the first pass kept. From steps/05/cipt/goals.py:

dominates(a, b) is true when a is at least as good as b on both goals and better on one, each comparison within that goal's slack in KEEP. The frontier comes out in the order the plans were found, from the best NPV to the most risk removed.

cipt/diagnose.py is new and is the stub for the lesson Why it failed, why it was deferred: a diagnostics layer. No code of this step imports it, and in this lesson's practice it is already this stub.

Five Points Show Where Risk Gets Expensive

frontier_small.py asks steps/05 for five points on GridCap small (a fictional utility):

The last column is the price of the next step: from the NPV end, $891k more risk removed costs $112k of NPV, $0.13 per dollar of risk. The last step costs $9.07 per dollar. A planner reads it at once: the first three points are cheap, and the risk end is not worth its price unless risk is the mandate.

The risk end is worth $18,878k here, against $18,559k for the step-04 risk plan. Both remove the same $6,827.9k of risk; the lexicographic second solve found $319k more NPV among the plans that tie on risk. That is the weak point of lesson AUGMECON: avoiding weakly efficient points, on real data.

The same program runs the weighted sum for comparison:

The weights found four plans the five-point grid did not, (24,069, 5,177.5) among them: five floors are a sample of the frontier, not all of it. A planner who wants more points asks for more, at two solves each. What the weights cannot give is a plan for a stated floor: no weight in the list answers "at least $5,000k of risk".

A Floor Is a Request Setting, So the Planners Can Ask for It

risk_floor lives in the request, so a planner sets it beside the export and gets one plan, by the ordinary solve. From steps/05/tests/test_goals.py, on three projects where any two fit:

test_tied_npv_end_takes_the_most_risk gives two projects the same NPV and very different risk; only one fits. A single NPV solve may return either. The test passes only if the second solve picks the riskier asset's project, whichever the first solve returned. Every test compares a list ([] == [...]) rather than indexing into it, so the stub's empty frontier fails on an assertion instead of an IndexError.

Two Goals Cost One File and One Reordering

The model file did not grow: the change there was the order of two blocks. goals.py holds the method, and it only calls build, add, maximize and solve_built, the same calls a planner's script could make. frontier is now the most-branched function, with its loops, comprehensions and guards; it is a candidate for splitting if it grows again.

Problem statement

The planning lead read the two plans of the lesson before and asked for something in between:

The NPV plan leaves too much risk on the table, and the risk plan gives up $5.7M of NPV. Show us the options in between: for each extra bit of risk removed, how much NPV does it cost? And let us set a floor on risk removed and get the best NPV above it.

You start from the planner that values each start by NPV and by risk removed. Give the planners the frontier: plans from the best NPV to the most risk removed, with plans in between, none of which is beaten on both goals by another, and where two plans tie on one goal, the one that does better on the other. Let the export set a floor on risk removed, and plan the best NPV above it.

cipt/goals.py already defines Point, one frontier plan with its NPV and risk removed. Two plans whose NPVs differ by at most 0.5 count as the same point, and only the first found is kept; dominated points are dropped after that. Every NPV in the export is a whole $k.

Run's report gains a section: after the plan, main.py prints an empty line, the line NPV against risk removed:, then one line per frontier point of the demo export, from the best NPV to the most risk removed: NPV $<npv>k risk removed $<risk>k funds <ids>, with two spaces before NPV and between the parts, NPV in whole $k and risk with one decimal (both with thousands commas), and the funded project ids in id order, separated by a comma and a space.

cipt/diagnose.py is the next lesson's stub, as the reference has it; no code of this step imports it, so leave it as it is.

Submit runs every test in tests/, the earlier steps' included, and compares Run's report with the expected one.

Blueprint

Write each method below. The tests check each one by itself and the whole program end to end, so you may change any other part of the code as you see fit.

best in cipt/goals.py

  • Contract. Maximizes the goal named first ('npv' or 'risk'), with an optional floor on the risk removed as a row named risk_floor; then, holding the first goal at its best within KEEP, maximizes the other. Returns the second solve's Plan, or the first one's when it is not optimal.
  • Modeling idea. A lexicographic second solve keeps the first goal and takes the most of the other, so no point is beaten by a plan equal on the first goal.
  • Taught in 8.2.1 Hierarchical Objectives with Tolerances.

dominates in cipt/goals.py

  • Contract. True when point a is at least as good as b on both goals and better on one, each comparison within the slack KEEP gives that goal.
  • Modeling idea. A point is on the frontier when no other point dominates it.
  • Taught in 8.3.2 AUGMECON: Avoiding Weakly Efficient Points.

frontier in cipt/goals.py

  • Contract. Returns up to points non-dominated points, from the best NPV to the most risk removed: the two ends, then best NPV for risk floors spread evenly between the ends' risk. Drops a point whose NPV repeats one already found, and any dominated point. Empty when either end is not optimal.
  • Modeling idea. The epsilon-constraint method: maximize one goal while the other must reach a floor, for a series of floors.
  • Taught in 8.3.1 The ε-Constraint Method.

risk_floor in cipt/rules.py

  • Contract. Adds a row risk_floor (the plan removes at least the request's risk_floor) when the request sets one, and nothing otherwise.
  • Modeling idea. A floor the planners set is one more named row over the risk expression.
  • Taught in 8.3.1 The ε-Constraint Method.

solve_built in cipt/solve.py

  • Contract. Solves a Built the caller may have changed after build (a new objective, an extra row) and returns its Plan.
  • Modeling idea. Solving a model you changed is a separate step from building it.
  • Taught in 14.2.5 Two Goals: Risk Against NPV.

build in cipt/model.py

  • Contract. Sets the NPV and risk expressions on the Built before the rules run, so a rule may name them.
  • Modeling idea. A rule over a goal needs the goal's expression to exist first.
  • Taught in 14.2.5 Two Goals: Risk Against NPV.

load_request in cipt/data.py

  • Contract. Also reads the request's optional risk_floor, in $k; None when the export has none.
  • Modeling idea. The floor is a request setting, so the planners can ask for it in the export.
  • Taught in 14.2.5 Two Goals: Risk Against NPV.

Summary


  • One objective gives one end of the trade. The step-04 code could not ask for a plan between the ends, and settled ties at each end by chance.
  • A floor on risk turns the trade into a series of NPV solves (ε-constraint), each finished by a lexicographic second solve.
  • Five points show where risk gets expensive: $0.13 of NPV per $1 of risk at first, $9.07 at the end, on GridCap small.
  • A floor is a request setting, so planners ask for it without code.

Further reading: MathOpt model.py (OR-Tools source) (maximize replaces the objective of a built model, which the second solve relies on); MathOpt expressions.py (OR-Tools source) (evaluate_expression, which gives each plan both of its values).


Challenges


  1. Ask for nine points instead of five. Predict how many survive the dominance filter, then run it.
  2. The planners want a floor on NPV and the most risk above it. Which two lines of best change?
  3. Why is keep_npv's slack 0.5 and not 0? Construct a case where 0 would make the second solve infeasible on a solver that returns 24,259.9999.


Design Note: A Method Outside the Model


goals.py builds no rows of its own kind; it adds ordinary rows and changes the objective of a model that build made. Putting the frontier inside build (a flag that returns many models) would have mixed the question "what are the rules" with "what are we asking". Keeping methods outside means a new question (a frontier on NPV against capex, say) is a new function in a new file, and model.py does not change.
