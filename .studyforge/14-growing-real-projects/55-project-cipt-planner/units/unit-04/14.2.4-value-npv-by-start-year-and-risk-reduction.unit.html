  16. Value by start year

Finance and the asset team both had comments on the step-03 plan:

Finance: you value every project at its earliest-start NPV, whatever year you start it. A project started in 2030 is worth less than the same project started in 2027; we sent you the NPV for every start year. Use it.

Asset management: NPV is not why we replace old equipment. Each project lowers the failure risk of one asset. We want to see how much risk a plan removes, and a plan that removes the most of it.

The Step-03 Plan Claims Value Its Starts Don't Have

value_by_start.py takes the step-03 plan of GridCap small (a fictional utility) and prices each funded project at finance's NPV for the year it actually starts:

The model claimed $2,199k that the plan does not have. The lesson Many years: start years, windows and cash-flow spreading left this gap open: $v_i$ did not depend on $t$, so a late start cost the model nothing and eleven projects drifted late. The code cannot do better because Project keeps one number, value, and the export's NPV by year is never read. Risk is not in the code at all.

A Coefficient per Variable Prices Each Start Year

The model already has one variable per project and start year, so the fix is a coefficient per variable: $\text{npv}_{i,t}$ from finance (lesson Precomputing NPV coefficients by start year) on $x_{i,t}$. Risk gets the same shape: $r_{i,t}$, the risk a start in year $t$ removes. Both are computed before the model is built, in a new file, because they are data about projects, not rows.

For $r_{i,t}$ the design question was where risk comes from:

ChoiceForAgainst
a risk score column in the exportno codesomeone else's formula, untestable here; the asset team would have to rerun it for every start year
the asset's predicted RUL as a hard deadlinesimplea deadline throws away how fast risk grows (lesson Remaining useful life: what an ML model actually hands you)
PoF from the asset's Weibull curve times CoF, summed over the years after the work (chosen)every number traceable to the export's asset columns; lessons Probability of failure and hazard curves (Weibull) to Risk reduction from an intervention, year by yeara formula the planner owns and must test

The annual probability of failure (PoF) of an asset aged $a$ at the start of a year, given it has not failed yet, from its Weibull shape $\beta$ and scale $\eta$ (lesson Probability of failure and hazard curves (Weibull)):

Its risk in year $y$ is $\text{pof}(a_0 + y - y_0) \times \text{CoF}$, with $a_0$ the asset's age in the plan's first year $y_0$ (the export's age column, read as Asset.age) and CoF its consequence of failure in $k (lesson Consequence of failure and risk = PoF × CoF). A start in $t$ finishes the work at the end of year $t + d_i - 1$, so it removes the risk of every year from $t + d_i$ to the plan's last year (lesson Risk reduction from an intervention, year by year). Risk after the plan's last year is not counted: the planner compares plans over the same years.

For P022, a three-year tower set repair on a 35-year-old asset:

A 2029 start finishes in 2031 and leaves no year inside the plan to remove risk from. NPV and risk both fall with a later start, but not in the same proportion, and that difference is what the next lesson trades.

The Model

The model of the lesson Business rules: must-dos, bundles, parents and alternatives, with the objective changed. The planners choose which measure to maximize with a setting in the request, objective, "npv" (the default) or "risk".

Sets. $P$, $Y$, $T_i$, $M$, $\mathcal{G}$, $\mathcal{B}$, $C$ as in the lesson Business rules: must-dos, bundles, parents and alternatives.

Parameters. $c_{i,k}$, $B_y$ as before ($k); $\text{npv}_{i,t}$, the NPV of project $i$ started in $t$ ($k), from the export; $r_{i,t}$, the risk it removes, computed as above ($k); $L_c$, the lags.

Variables. $x_{i,t} \in \{0, 1\}$ for every $i \in P$, $t \in T_i$.

The Change, File by File

cipt/value.py. Before: the stub of the lesson Business rules: must-dos, bundles, parents and alternatives, every function returning 0.0. After, from steps/04/cipt/value.py:

The rest of value.py turns that curve into the coefficient $r_{i,t}$ of every start, and the page leaves its code to the practice. Three functions, each one step of the section A Coefficient per Variable Prices Each Start Year above:

  • annual_risk(request, project, year) is the risk of the project's asset in one calendar year if nothing is done: $\text{pof}(a_0 + y - y_0) \times \text{CoF}$, in $k. The asset is project.asset (the Asset record below), $a_0$ is its age and $y_0$ is the plan's first year, request.years[0].
  • risk_reduction(request, project, start) is $r_{i,t}$, and its docstring says what it counts: "Risk removed by a start in start: every year after the work ends, to the plan's last year." That is the annual risks of every year from $t + d_i$ to the plan's last year, request.years[-1], added up. A start whose work ends in the plan's last year removes no risk inside the plan, so it is 0.0.
  • coefficients(request) prices every start before the model is built: a dict whose key is (project_id, t) for every project and every t in request.starts(p), and whose value is the pair ($\text{npv}_{i,t}$, $r_{i,t}$), with the NPV read from p.npv[t].

coefficients is keyed exactly like built.x, so the model can zip them without a lookup that might miss.

Maximizing NPV and Maximizing Risk Give Different Plans

The NPV plan is worth $351k more than the step-03 plan's starts were ($23,909k), with the same rules and budgets: starting the right projects early is worth money the old objective could not see. The risk plan removes 84% more risk and gives up $5,701k of NPV. It funds four more projects within the same budgets. Neither plan is wrong; they answer different questions, and the planners asked both.

On the demo export, the report now shows NPV for each actual start: P001 starts in 2029 and is worth $1,077k there, not the $1,386k the earlier steps printed.

A Formula Gets a Test With a Value You Can Compute by Hand

With $\beta = 1$ the Weibull curve is the exponential distribution, which has no memory: every year's PoF is $1 - e^{-1/\eta}$ whatever the age. The tests use that to pin the formula to a number a reader can check. From steps/04/tests/test_value.py:

The first line is the step-03 model claiming 500 for project A, whose 500 is only real in 2027, a year with no money. Three of the "before" answers are 0.0: the stub's honest wrong answer, which is why the step-03 snapshot shipped one.

Value Added a File, Not Branches

The change touched six files but value.py holds all of the arithmetic, in four functions that take plain values and return numbers. model.py grew by six lines and has no formula in it. The loader is again the most-branched function: every new column is read and checked there.

Practice. This lesson's project practice starts from steps/03, the whole package. It is graded by the tests in the practice's tests/ folder and by a check that main.py, which plans the demo export, prints what the practice's expected/report.txt holds.

Finance: you value every project at its earliest-start NPV, whatever year you start it. A project started in 2030 is worth less than the same project started in 2027; we sent you the NPV for every start year. Use it.

Asset management: NPV is not why we replace old equipment. Each project lowers the failure risk of one asset. We want to see how much risk a plan removes, and a plan that removes the most of it.

You start from the rule-keeping planner of the lesson before. Price every start: its NPV is the figure finance quotes for that start year, and the risk it removes is the asset's yearly risk, failure probability times the cost of a failure, over every year after the work ends to the end of the plan. The failure probability comes from the asset's Weibull curve. The export says which of the two the plan maximizes, NPV unless it says risk, and the plan and its report carry both.

The export gives each asset's age in the plan's first year; the asset is one year older in each later year. The report changes in three ways, every amount with thousands commas:

  • the funded line keeps its form, funded <n> of <all> projects, capex $<capex>k of $<budgets>k, value $<value>k, but its value is now the plan's objective, what the model maximized, in whole $k (the NPV of the actual starts when the export asks for NPV);
  • a new line follows it: NPV $<npv>k, risk reduced $<risk>k (maximized: <objective>), NPV in whole $k and risk with one decimal;
  • each funded project's line ends capex <capex> npv <npv> <name>: the label npv replaces value, and the amount, right-aligned in 6 characters, is the NPV of the year the project starts.

cipt/goals.py is the next lesson's stub, as the reference has it; no code of this step imports it, so leave it as it is.

Run executes main.py, which plans the demo export and prints the report; expected/report.txt is the report it should print. Submit runs every test in tests/, the earlier steps' included, and compares Run's report with the expected one.

Blueprint

Write each method below. The tests check each one by itself and the whole program end to end, so you may change any other part of the code as you see fit.

annual_pof in cipt/value.py

  • Contract. Returns the chance that an asset of this age fails within the next year, given that it has not failed yet, on a Weibull curve with shape beta and scale eta.
  • Modeling idea. The conditional one-year PoF is 1 - exp(H(age) - H(age + 1)), with the cumulative hazard H(a) = (a / eta) ** beta.
  • Taught in 7.3.1 Probability of Failure and Hazard Curves (Weibull).

annual_risk in cipt/value.py

  • Contract. Returns the expected failure cost, in $k, of the project's asset in a calendar year if nothing is done: the consequence of failure times the PoF at the asset's age in that year. The export's age is the asset's age in the plan's first year, so in year y the age is age + y - first year.
  • Modeling idea. Risk is PoF times CoF, and the age grows by one each year from the plan's first year.
  • Taught in 7.3.3 Consequence of Failure and Risk = PoF × CoF.

risk_reduction in cipt/value.py

  • Contract. Returns the risk removed by a start in a given year: the asset's annual risk summed over every year after the work ends, to the plan's last year.
  • Modeling idea. Work removes the risk of the years after it is done; a later start removes fewer years.
  • Taught in 7.3.4 Risk Reduction From an Intervention, Year by Year.

coefficients in cipt/value.py

  • Contract. Returns, for every project and start year the model has a variable for, the pair (NPV of that start, risk reduction of that start).
  • Modeling idea. Price each start before the model is built, so each variable gets its own coefficient.
  • Taught in 7.2.4 Precomputing NPV Coefficients by Start Year.

build in cipt/model.py

  • Contract. Keeps the plan's NPV and its risk removed as two expressions on the Built, and maximizes the one the request's objective names ('npv' or 'risk').
  • Modeling idea. Two measures over the same variables are two linear expressions; the objective picks one.
  • Taught in 14.2.4 Value: NPV by Start Year and Risk Reduction.

read_plan in cipt/solve.py

  • Contract. Also returns the plan's NPV and risk removed, evaluated from the two expressions at the solution.
  • Modeling idea. The plan reports both measures whichever one was maximized.
  • Taught in 4.2.2 Reading SolveResult: Termination, Primal and Dual Solutions, Statistics.

load_request in cipt/data.py

  • Contract. Also reads each project's NPV for every start year and its asset (id, age, Weibull shape and scale, consequence of failure), and the request's objective, 'npv' by default; refuses any other objective.
  • Modeling idea. The export already carries NPV by start year and asset health; read them where they are.
  • Taught in 7.2.4 Precomputing NPV Coefficients by Start Year.

render in cipt/report.py

  • Contract. Changes the report in three ways. The funded line's value $<value>k is now the plan's objective, what the model maximized (the NPV of the actual starts when the objective is 'npv'). After it comes the line NPV $<npv>k, risk reduced $<risk>k (maximized: <objective>), NPV in whole $k and risk with one decimal, both with thousands commas. Each funded project's line shows npv and the NPV of the year the project starts, right-aligned in 6 characters, where it showed value and the earliest-start value.
  • Modeling idea. A report shows the measure that was not maximized as well as the one that was.
  • Taught in 14.2.4 Value: NPV by Start Year and Risk Reduction.

Summary


  • The step-03 plan claims value its starts don't have: $26,108k claimed, $23,909k real.
  • A coefficient per variable prices each start year: $\text{npv}_{i,t}$ and $r_{i,t}$ on $x_{i,t}$, both computed in value.py before the model is built.
  • Risk removed is PoF times CoF over the years after the work, from the asset's Weibull curve.
  • Maximizing NPV and maximizing risk give different plans: $24,260k and $3,709k against $18,559k and $6,828k.

Design Note: Keep Both Measures on Built


The model maximizes one measure, but Built holds both, and every plan reports both. A plan that reports only what it maximized hides what it gave up: the risk plan's $5,701k of NPV would be invisible. Holding both expressions also makes the next lesson cheap, since a frontier only adds a row on one of them and maximizes the other.
