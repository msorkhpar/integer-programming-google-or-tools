  16. Calendars and optional work

The risk-ordered schedule of the lesson Risk grows while a circuit waits: the objective changes went to the field. Three answers came back the same day, from three people:

Crew supervisor: The River circuits run through bottom land. Until the ground dries, around week 8, we can't get bucket trucks in. And K1 is at training in weeks 10 and 11; K2 is held back for storm duty in weeks 14 to 17.

Environmental lead: About half of the Hills circuits cross nesting habitat. No work there from week 6 to week 13.

Compliance: C05 and C16 are overdue on their trim cycle. They are not optional this year.

Each answer is a calendar: weeks when a circuit may be worked (an access window), weeks when a crew cannot work, and circuits that must be trimmed whatever their risk. Every other circuit stays optional work: trimmed if it is worth its place, otherwise left for next season at the risk the lesson Risk grows while a circuit waits: the objective changes prices. (GridCap and its seasons are fictional, generated by the project's seeded generator.) This lesson takes the project from steps/04 to steps/05.

The supervisor speaks of the small season, which has two crews. The generator writes the crews' calendar for any number of crews: K1 is off for training in weeks 10 and 11, and the season's last crew, when there is more than one, is held back for storm duty in weeks 14 to 17. That is K2 in the small season and K6 in the medium one; the tiny season's single crew has only the training weeks.

The Plan Ignores Every Calendar, Because the Model Has No Word for One

Seven broken rules in nine trims, and a compliance circuit left out. None of it is a bug in the sense of wrong code: the model was never told. The steps/04 plan above was solved with repeatable settings, as in the lesson Risk grows while a circuit waits: the objective changes, so the list repeats from run to run.

Each Calendar Is a Constraint Kind CP-SAT Already Has

Three rules, and for each the design question is which CP-SAT construct states it. Lesson Cumulative resources: crews and outage windows met two of them on GridCap's outage windows; the options:

RuleChosenAlternative, and why not
A trim lies inside one access windowthe start's domain is the union of the starts that fit a window: $s \in \bigcup_j [a_j,\, b_j - w + 1]$a Boolean per window and enforced rows a_j <= s and s + w - 1 <= b_j: more variables to say what a domain says for free
A crew is off some weeksa fixed interval for each span off, added to that crew's NoOverlapone start bound per trim and span: a pair of rows per trim, crew and span, and wrong if a trim may fit before the span
A must is trimmed$t_c = 1$forcing the presence of one crew option: but which crew? $t_c$ already says "some crew"

The window and the time off are the same idea from two sides. A window shrinks where a trim may start; time off occupies the crew's line so nothing else may sit there. Both are data the model reads, not code paths, and both are invisible to every other row.

What about a window too short for its trim, like a 7-week trim in a 6-week window? Then that window contributes no starts, and if no window fits, the start domain is empty. Such a circuit gets no crew options at all and waits for next season, exactly like a trim longer than the season in the lesson A trim takes weeks on one crew: from MILP to CP-SAT intervals. And a must circuit that cannot be scheduled makes the whole season infeasible: no plan keeps every rule. That is the right answer, and the lesson Planner overrides and why a plan failed is about saying why.

The Model

The lesson Risk grows while a circuit waits: the objective changes's model with three changes, in full:

Sets. $C$, circuits; $K$, crews; weeks $0, \dots, H - 1$; $M \subseteq C$, the must circuits ← this lesson; for each circuit $c$, its windows $W_c$, each a span $[a, b]$ of weeks (no windows means $[0, H - 1]$) ← this lesson; for each crew $k$, its spans off $O_k$ ← this lesson.

Parameters. $\text{cost}_c$, $B$ ($k); $w_c$ (weeks); $R_c(e)$ ($k); $D = 26$.

Variables. $t_c \in \{0,1\}$; $p_{c,k} \in \{0,1\}$; $s_{c,k} \in S_c = \bigcup_{[a,b] \in W_c,\ b - w_c + 1 \ge a} \{a, \dots, b - w_c + 1\}$ ← this lesson: the window is the start's domain; the optional interval $I_{c,k} = [s_{c,k}, s_{c,k} + w_c)$; $e_c \in \{w_c..H\} \cup \{H + D\}$; $\rho_c$. A circuit with $S_c = \emptyset$ has no $p, s, I$, so $t_c = 0$.

Objective.

Constraints.

The window rule (window$_c$ in the code's comments) is not a row: it is the domain $S_c$.

Every Calendar Kept, at a Price

calendar_plan.py plans the small season with steps/05 (with WORKERS set to 1; the plan is proved optimal well inside the time limit, so it repeats) and runs the same checker:

Every rule holds. K1 is idle for its training weeks and starts C04 right after; K2 does C01 before its storm duty and the must C05 after it. C16 (a 7-week must) runs early on K2, and every River circuit starts at week 8 or later.

The calendars cost risk. The steps/04 plan carried $3,989k without them, and it was a real plan of that model, so the best plan without calendars carries at most $3,989k; the best plan with them carries $4,571k (OPTIMAL). The calendars cost at least $582k of risk over the season, and the plan drops from nine trims to eight. That number is worth showing the people who set the calendars: it is what the training weeks, the nesting closure and the wet ground cost in risk.

The calendars also made the model easier: the small season, which steps/04 could not prove in 20 units of deterministic time, is proved optimal here. Narrow domains and blocked weeks leave fewer orders to try.

Seven Tests, One per Calendar Case

The others: a trim inside a single window; a window shorter than the trim, which leaves the circuit for next season with status OPTIMAL; a crew off weeks 0 to 3, so its only trim starts at week 4; and two musts that cannot both fit, which must come back INFEASIBLE with no trims. Problem statement

The risk-ordered schedule went to the field, and three answers came back the same day:

Crew supervisor: The River circuits run through bottom land. Until the ground dries, around week 8, we can't get bucket trucks in. And K1 is at training in weeks 10 and 11; K2 is held back for storm duty in weeks 14 to 17.

Environmental lead: About half of the Hills circuits cross nesting habitat. No work there from week 6 to week 13.

Compliance: C05 and C16 are overdue on their trim cycle. They are not optional this year.

The supervisor speaks of the small season's two crews. The generator gives K1 the training weeks 10 and 11 in every season and, when a season has more than one crew, gives its last crew the storm duty in weeks 14 to 17: K2 in the small season, K6 in the medium; the tiny season's one crew has only the training weeks.

You start from the risk-ordered scheduler of the lesson before. Read three calendars, and have the generator draw them: each circuit's access windows, each crew's weeks off, and the circuits that must be trimmed this season. A trim lies wholly inside one window of its circuit, never across the closed weeks between two; a circuit whose windows are all too short for its trim waits for next season; no crew works in its weeks off; every must circuit is trimmed, and a season whose musts cannot all be trimmed is infeasible. The report marks each crew's weeks off.

The report. Each crew's week chart marks the crew's weeks off with x (a trim never covers one); the rest of every line is as before.

Run executes main.py, which plans the tiny season (six circuits of GridCap, a fictional utility, drawn by vegcrew/generate.py from seed 54) and prints the report; expected/report.txt is the report it should print. Submit runs every test in tests/, the earlier steps' included, and compares Run's report with the expected one.

Summary


  • The plan ignored every calendar, because the model had no word for one. A checker written from the rules found seven broken rules in the steps/04 plan.
  • Each calendar is a constraint kind CP-SAT already has. A window is the start's domain, time off is a fixed interval in the crew's NoOverlap, a must is $t_c = 1$; all other work stays optional.
  • Every calendar kept, at a price: OPTIMAL at $4,571k, at least $582k more than without calendars, and one trim fewer.
  • Seven tests, one per calendar case, each with a single right week.

Design Note: A Window Belongs to the Data, a Domain to the Model


The inventory says "weeks 8 to 23", inclusive, because that is how people speak. CP-SAT wants the set of allowed starts for a trim of a given length. The translation happens in one function, start_domain, which knows about trim lengths and the season's end and nothing else. Every other part of the code (the generator, the loader, the report, the checker in the tests) speaks in the people's terms. Keep such translations in one place, small and named, and an off-by-one in a window is a one-line fix with one test to prove it.
