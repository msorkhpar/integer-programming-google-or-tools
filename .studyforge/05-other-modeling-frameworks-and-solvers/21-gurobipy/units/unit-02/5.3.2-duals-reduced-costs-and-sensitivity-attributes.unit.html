GridCap's finance partner has read lesson Explaining a dual value to a finance partner and asks the modeling team three questions about next year's capital plan: what one more $k of the 2030 budget is worth, up to how much extra the answer holds, and how much a deferred project's NPV would have to rise before the plan takes it. In lesson Reading SolveResult: termination, primal and dual solutions, statistics MathOpt answered the first with dual_values; MathOpt has no call for the other two, and lesson Shadow prices, reduced costs and ranging from a solver got them from HiGHS's own interface. Gurobi answers all three with attributes of the solved model. This lesson reads them on the LP relaxation of GridCap small's selection model, checks each against a re-solve, and shows what Gurobi offers, and refuses, for a MIP. GridCap is a fictional utility, and its numbers are teaching data.

The words come from lessons The dual LP and what its variables price–Shadow prices, reduced costs and ranging from a solver: a row's dual value (or shadow price) is how much the optimal objective changes per unit of extra right-hand side; a variable's reduced cost is how much the objective changes per unit the variable is pushed away from the bound it sits at; and ranging gives the interval over which each of those numbers holds. All three are properties of an optimal basic LP solution, the vertex a simplex method ends on (lesson Simplex as walking between vertices (a tiny tableau implementation)).

The Model

The selection model of lesson Models, attributes and the matrix API with its integrality dropped, the LP relaxation (lesson Yes/no decisions, and why rounding an LP fails): each $x_i$ may take any value from 0 to 1.

Sets. $P$ the candidate projects; $Y$ the years 2027–2031; $M \subseteq P$ the must-do projects; $\mathcal{G}$ the alternative groups; $C \subseteq P$ the child projects, with parent $p(c)$.

Parameters. $v_i$, project $i$'s NPV at its earliest start ($k); $c_{i,y}$, its spend in year $y$ at that start ($k); $B_y$, the budget of year $y$ ($k).

Variables. $0 \le x_i \le 1$ for every $i \in P$ (the MIP of the last section has $x_i \in \{0, 1\}$).

Objective. $\max \sum_{i \in P} v_i\, x_i$.

Constraints.

build_selection(env, small, integer=False) builds it; the rows are named as in lesson Models, attributes and the matrix API (budget[2030], alt_G001, …). The first sections use a smaller LP, stated where it appears.

In gurobipy, with every LP result read as an attribute:

The numbers are the ones GLOP returned in lesson Reading SolveResult: termination, primal and dual solutions, statistics, under Gurobi's names:

The basis codes are numbers: a row's CBasis is 0 if the row is basic (its slack is in the basis) and $-1$ if not; a variable's VBasis is 0 if basic, $-1$ at its lower bound, $-2$ at its upper bound, and $-3$ superbasic (nonbasic yet strictly between its bounds). Complementary slackness (lesson Weak and strong duality, complementary slackness) is visible: the row with slack, c2, has price 0, and the two tight rows have prices.

These attributes exist only when they make sense. The attribute reference says Pi and RC are available for continuous, convex models, and CBasis, VBasis and every SA range below only for basic solutions. Reading one that is not there raises an error, as the last section shows.

The Sign of Pi Follows the Model's Sense

The same LP can be written as a minimization of $-f$. The plan cannot change, but the prices change sign:

Both are right. Pi is the rate of change of the objective as written: one more unit of c1 raises $f$ by 0.6667, so it lowers $-f$ by 0.6667. The linear constraint attribute page states the rule:

Sense of the model$\le$ row$\ge$ row$=$ row
minimize$\pi \le 0$$\pi \ge 0$any sign
maximize$\pi \ge 0$$\pi \le 0$any sign

So before quoting a price to finance, check the sense: for GridCap's maximized NPV, a budget row's Pi is the NPV gained per extra $k, and it is never negative.

A variable's reduced cost follows from the prices. For a column $j$ with objective coefficient $c_j$ and coefficients $a_{ij}$ in the rows,

the value the variable brings, less what the rows it uses charge at their prices. For $x_2$: $4 - (5 \times 0.6667 + 6 \times 0 + 1 \times 3.3333) = -2.6667$. In a maximization at optimum, a variable at its lower bound has $\text{RC} \le 0$ (bringing it in would not pay) and one at its upper bound $\text{RC} \ge 0$; a basic variable has $\text{RC} = 0$.

Problem statement

A furniture workshop's LP has been solved, and the team has its row prices (Pi) but wants to check the reduced costs and whether the basis is optimal, by hand. For an LP with objective coefficients $c_j$, row coefficients $a_{ij}$ and row prices $\pi_i$, the reduced cost of column $j$ is

Write two functions.

reduced_costs(c, A, pi): c is a list of objective coefficients, A a list of rows (each a list with one coefficient per column), pi a list of row prices. Return the list of reduced costs, one per column.

wrong_signs(rc, where, maximize, tol=1e-9): where[j] says where variable $j$ sits: "basic", "lower" (at its lower bound) or "upper" (at its upper bound). At an optimal basis:

where[j]maximizeminimize
"basic"$\lvert\text{RC}_j\rvert \le$ tol$\lvert\text{RC}_j\rvert \le$ tol
"lower"$\text{RC}_j \le$ tol$\text{RC}_j \ge -$tol
"upper"$\text{RC}_j \ge -$tol$\text{RC}_j \le$ tol

Return the sorted indices $j$ that break their rule (an empty list means the basis passes). No Gurobi license is needed. About fifteen lines.

A Price Holds Only Inside SARHSLow to SARHSUp


On GridCap small's LP relaxation, Pi gives each year's budget price, and two more row attributes give its range: SARHSLow and SARHSUp, the lowest and highest right-hand side for which the current optimal basis stays optimal. Inside that interval the price is constant and the objective changes by exactly $\pi \times \Delta$:






The objective, 23,669.49, and the prices are the ones GLOP gave through MathOpt in lesson Reading SolveResult: termination, primal and dual solutions, statistics (0.6475, 0.5765, 0, 1.3817, 0), and the module's tests check every price against GLOP's. The new columns answer finance's second question. One more $k in 2030 is worth $1.38k of NPV, and that holds from a 2030 budget of 7,620 up to 9,300. 2029 and 2031 have money to spare, so their price is 0, and no amount of extra budget changes that (the upper end is infinite); cutting them too far would (below 8,070 and 5,030).


The program checks the 2030 range by re-solving, once inside it, once at its end and once past it:






Up to 9,300 the gain is the price times the change, to the cent. Past it, the 300 extra $k earn $1{,}331.92 - 953.35 = 378.57$, about 1.26 per $k instead of 1.38: the basis changed, and with it the price. That is the answer finance needs: "about $1.38k per extra $k, for up to $690k more" ($9{,}300 - 8{,}610$), and a new solve beyond that. Two limits from lesson Shadow prices, reduced costs and ranging from a solver still apply: a range assumes one change at a time (moving two budgets together can cross a breakpoint neither crosses alone), and it describes the LP relaxation, not the yes/no plan (lesson Explaining a dual value to a finance partner and the last section).

Problem statement

Objective Ranging Says How Far a Project's NPV Can Move


The third question, how much a project's NPV must change before the plan changes, is answered by two variable attributes: SAObjLow and SAObjUp, the range of the objective coefficient over which the current basis stays optimal. Read next to X, VBasis and RC:






Four cases, one per row:


  • P005 is at its upper bound ($-2$), fully funded. Its NPV could fall to 0 and the LP would still fund it, and any rise only confirms it. Its reduced cost equals its NPV, 576: P005 spends $1,510k only in 2029, whose price is 0, so the rows charge it nothing.
  • P002 is at its lower bound ($-1$), deferred. Its NPV of 386 would have to exceed 937.9 before the LP funds any of it, and the gap, $937.9 - 386 = 551.9$, is its reduced cost with the sign dropped. That is the "why deferred" number lesson Why selected, why deferred: forcing in and out builds on.
  • P024 is basic and fractional (0.729): the LP funds 73% of it, and the basis holds while its NPV stays between 388.2 and 522.2.
  • P001 is basic at 1.000. A basic variable can sit at a bound; the LP is then degenerate (lesson Infeasible, unbounded and degenerate LPs), and its range, 849.1 upward, is for this basis.


Where does P002's reduced cost come from? Its column has two nonzero coefficients, and the program prints what each row charges at its price:






getCol returns a variable's column: the rows it appears in and its coefficients there. P002 spends $460k in 2027, charged at 0.6475, and it belongs to alternative group G001, whose single seat the LP prices at 640.06. The sum, 937.93, is SAObjUp, the NPV P002 would need. The budget alone would have said 297.87; the alternative group is most of the reason P002 waits.


A MIP Has No Duals; fixed() Prices the Plan It Found


Now the real model, with $x_i \in \{0, 1\}$:






A MIP's best value jumps when a budget crosses a project's cost, so there is no rate of change to report, and Gurobi reports none: Pi (and RC, SARHSLow and the rest) are for continuous models only. MathOpt said the same with has_dual_feasible_solution() == False (lesson Reading SolveResult: termination, primal and dual solutions, statistics).


What Gurobi does offer is Model.fixed(): a new model, continuous, in which every integer variable is fixed at its value in the MIP's solution, as the Python reference describes it. Solved, it is an LP, and it has prices:






The fixed LP has the MIP's value, 22,532, and duals, but read them carefully. Every budget price is 0: with all 40 variables fixed ($\text{LB} = \text{UB}$), no budget can change the plan, so no budget is worth anything. Each variable's reduced cost is its whole NPV, because the bound, not any row, holds it in place. In this selection model, where every variable is a binary, the fixed LP prices only the decisions already taken. Fixed-integer duals mean something when the model also has continuous variables (spend, carry-over, draws on a credit line) that stay free once the binaries are fixed; lessons Why a MILP has no duals and Fixed-integer LP duals, and when they mean something take that up, and say when such prices can be trusted.


Summary


  • An optimal LP carries Pi, RC, Slack and a basis. Pi and Slack on rows, RC on variables, CBasis and VBasis for the basis; on the sample LP they match GLOP's values in MathOpt.
  • The sign of Pi follows the model's sense. In a maximization a $\le$ row's price is $\ge 0$; minimize $-f$ and every sign flips. $\text{RC}_j = c_j - \sum_i a_{ij}\pi_i$.
  • A price holds only inside SARHSLow to SARHSUp. GridCap's 2030 budget is worth $1.38k per $k from 7,620 to 9,300; a re-solve confirmed the price to the cent inside and found less past it.
  • Objective ranging says how far a project's NPV can move. SAObjLow and SAObjUp; a deferred project needs SAObjUp, here its NPV less its reduced cost, and getCol shows which rows set it.
  • A MIP has no duals; fixed() prices the plan it found. Pi raises on a MIP. The fixed LP of an all-binary model prices the bounds, not the budgets.

Design Note: A Price Is a Sentence With a Range in It


Finance does not need Pi; it needs "each extra $k of the 2030 budget is worth about $1.38k, for up to $690k more, if nothing else changes". Every part of that sentence is an attribute: Pi, SARHSUp − RHS, and the one-change-at-a-time rule. A report that prints prices without ranges invites the reading "another $5M is worth $6.9M", which the re-solve here already disproved at $300k past the range. Lesson A binding-constraints report builds a binding-constraints report around exactly this sentence.
