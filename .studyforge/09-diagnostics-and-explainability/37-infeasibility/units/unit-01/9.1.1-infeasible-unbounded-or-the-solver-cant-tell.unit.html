It is Budget Lock week at GridCap (lesson The planning cycle: Initial, Pre-Opt, Post-Opt, Budget Lock): one scenario is about to become the plan of record. Finance has locked the 2028 budget at $6,000k, a contractor change leaves transmission 12 crew-months in each of 2030 and 2031, and the board wants P032 started in 2028 and P023 in the plan. The run comes back with no plan. In the same week a developer adds interest on carried-over money to another model, and that run also comes back with no plan. HiGHS gives the two runs different answers, and one of those answers is "infeasible or unbounded". Before anyone looks for the rule to blame, someone has to work out which of the two problems each model has. This lesson reads what a solver says when a model has no optimal plan, measures what five solvers say on the same small models, and settles the ambiguous answer. Section 9 uses this Budget Lock scenario throughout. GridCap is a fictional utility; none of its numbers are real.

The Model

The model is the timing model of lesson Start-time variables x(i,t) and timing windows with crew rows (lesson Deriving big-M from bounds, held hard as in lesson Bound tightening and valid inequalities) and the rule families planners touch at Budget Lock. lock_model.build(portfolio, scenario) builds it in MathOpt and gives every row a name. The names are the labels below, and the rest of the section talks about rows only by these names.

Sets. $P$ the projects; $Y = \{2027, \dots, 2031\}$ the years; $T_i \subseteq Y$ the start years project $i$ may use (its window); $M \subseteq P$ the must-do projects; $\mathcal{G}$ the alternative groups, each a set of projects; $C \subseteq P$ the child projects, with parent $p(c)$ and lag $L_c$ years; $Q$ the programs (transmission, distribution, vegetation), with $q(i)$ the program of project $i$; $R$ the regions, with $r(i)$ the region of project $i$; $F \subseteq P$ the projects the scenario forces in, and $F_1 \subseteq F$ those forced into one year $f_i$; $D \subseteq P$ the projects it forces out.

Parameters. $\text{npv}_{i,t}$, the NPV of project $i$ started in year $t$ ($k); $c_{i,k}$, what it spends in its $k$-th year of work, $k = 0, \dots, d_i - 1$, where $d_i$ is its number of years of work ($k; $c_{i,k} = 0$ for any other $k$); $\text{capex}_i = \sum_k c_{i,k}$; $B_y$, the budget of year $y$ ($k); $m_i$, the crew-months project $i$ needs in each year of work; $K_{q,y}$, the crew-months program $q$ has in year $y$; $\text{floor}_r$, the least region $r$ must receive over the plan ($k).

Variables. $x_{i,t} \in \{0, 1\}$ for every $i \in P$ and $t \in T_i$: project $i$ starts in year $t$.

A Termination Reason Is a Claim About the Model, Not About Your Plan

Lesson Reading SolveResult: termination, primal and dual solutions, statistics read result.termination: a reason (how the solve ended), a limit (which limit stopped it, if one did), and a problem_status with the solver's claims about the primal problem (your model) and the dual problem (lesson The dual LP and what its variables price; for a MIP, the dual of its LP relaxation). When there is no optimal plan, the reason is one of four:

TerminationReasonWhat the solver claims
INFEASIBLEno plan meets every row and bound: the primal problem has no feasible point
UNBOUNDEDthere are plans, and the objective can be made as good as you like (a ray: a direction you can move in forever, staying feasible and improving)
INFEASIBLE_OR_UNBOUNDEDone of the two, and the solver does not know which
NO_SOLUTION_FOUNDa limit stopped the search before any plan was found: nothing proven either way

problem_status says the same thing in pieces. primal_status and dual_status are each FEASIBLE, INFEASIBLE or UNDETERMINED, and primal_or_dual_infeasible is True when the solver knows that one of the two is infeasible but not which. Lesson Infeasible, unbounded and degenerate LPs showed the geometry: an infeasible LP has an empty region, and an unbounded LP has a region that stretches forever in an improving direction.

The two answers ask for different work. INFEASIBLE is about the data and the rules: some set of them cannot hold together, and the rest of this module finds which. UNBOUNDED is almost always a modeling bug, because no real plan is worth infinite money. Usually a bound or a row is missing. Treat INFEASIBLE_OR_UNBOUNDED as an unanswered question, not as either of the two.

Five Solvers Give Three Answers for the Same Unbounded Model

status_zoo.py builds three two-variable models, maximizing $x + y$ over $x, y \ge 0$. "infeasible" has rows $x + y \le 4$ and $x + y \ge 6$. "unbounded" has the single row $x - y \le 2$, so $x = y = t$ is feasible for every $t$. "both" has $x - y \ge 1$ and $x - y \le -1$, so the primal is infeasible, and so is its dual. Each model is solved as an LP and as a MIP, by every solver that takes it, with presolve (the pass that simplifies a model before the main algorithm, lesson Presolve: what the solver removes first) on and off:

An error is shown up to the end of its first clause; "..." marks a message that goes on. Read it by rows:

  • Every solver called the infeasible model INFEASIBLE, as an LP and as a MIP, with presolve on or off. On these models that answer was never wrong, and never hedged.
  • The unbounded model got three different answers. HiGHS and SCIP said UNBOUNDED for the LP. GLOP and PDLP said INFEASIBLE_OR_UNBOUNDED, and so did CP-SAT, which also gives no definite answer with presolve off. HiGHS said UNBOUNDED for the LP but INFEASIBLE_OR_UNBOUNDED for the same model with integer variables.
  • Switching presolve off settled it for GLOP and for HiGHS's MIP, both of which then said UNBOUNDED. The other columns show that "presolve off" is no general cure. PDLP refuses the parameter. SCIP with presolve off fails in OR-Tools while reading SCIP's answer, because the objective value of an unbounded solve is infinite. OR-Tools 9.15 raises that as an AttributeError, and the solver's own message, the one the table prints, is on the error's __context__ (lesson Common vs. solver-specific parameters, and what each solver rejects). CP-SAT's answer does not change.
  • "both" shows why a solver may have to hedge. Its primal and its dual are both infeasible. A solver that learns only "primal or dual is infeasible" cannot say which. GLOP with presolve said INFEASIBLE_OR_UNBOUNDED, and without presolve it said INFEASIBLE.

Why does presolve cause this? Some presolve steps use the objective: they fix a variable, or drop a row, because no optimal plan could need it. Such a step is safe only when an optimal plan exists. When the reduced model then turns out to have no plan, or to be unbounded, the solver cannot be sure the original model was the same. Switching presolve off, or only those steps, removes the doubt at the cost of a slower solve.

Gurobi Says INF_OR_UNBD Until Dual Reductions Are Off

Gurobi has a status for this case, INF_OR_UNBD (status 4), and a parameter that switches off exactly the presolve steps that use the objective in this way: dual reductions. Gurobi's parameter reference says to set DualReductions to 0 to get a definite answer after INF_OR_UNBD. gurobi_status.py solves the same three models in gurobipy, on the size-limited license mounted in the runner, with the default settings and then with dual reductions off:

With default settings, Gurobi hedged on five of the six models, the plainly infeasible ones too. With DualReductions=0 it gave a definite answer for every LP and for both infeasible MIPs. The unbounded MIP is a warning of its own: with dual reductions off, Gurobi 13 reported OPTIMAL with an objective of inf and x = 1e+30, which is Gurobi's "infinity". An OPTIMAL status does not by itself mean the plan is usable. Check that the objective is finite before you read the plan.

The two tables say the same thing about the method. A hedged answer comes from presolve. Asking again with the doubtful steps switched off settles it for most solvers and models, but not for all. The next section's method works on every solver that can say INFEASIBLE.

A Zero-Objective Copy Settles INFEASIBLE_OR_UNBOUNDED

A model with no objective cannot be unbounded: every plan scores 0, so every plan is optimal. Solve a copy of the model with its objective cleared. If the copy has a plan, the original has plans too, and since the solver hedged it must be unbounded. If the copy has none, the original is infeasible. diagnose.separate does this, and never edits the caller's model:

copy is mathopt.Model.from_model_proto(model.export_model()), the copy lesson Prioritized objectives with add_auxiliary_objective used, and verdict maps the termination reason to a word (the next sections show the cases where no word fits). Now the two broken models of this week. The second one is the developer's: finance pays 3% interest on money carried into the next year, so each year gets a carry variable $r_y \ge 0$ (continuous, with no upper bound) and the objective gains $0.03 \sum_y r_y$. Lesson Carry-over budgets and multi-year caps's cash row is $\text{spend}_y + r_y \le B_y + r_{y-1}$. The developer wrote it without the $+ r_y$ on the left:

HiGHS answered the Budget Lock scenario with INFEASIBLE, so separate needed no second solve. For the carry bug it hedged, as it did on the small unbounded MIP above, and the zero-objective copy found a plan, so the model is unbounded. The two models need different work: the lock needs the rest of this module, and the carry bug needs one missing term.

The copy answers only "is there any plan?". When it comes back INFEASIBLE, that is a full answer about the original model, because the rows and bounds are the same. When it hits a limit, the answer is "unknown", and separate says so rather than guess.

Capping Every Free Variable Names the Ones That Run Away

"Unbounded" says some direction improves forever, not which variables move along it. A solver can return that direction as a ray (result.has_ray(), ray_variable_values()). On the carry bug's LP relaxation (every $x$ continuous in $[0, 1]$), none of HiGHS, GLOP or SCIP returned one through MathOpt in OR-Tools 9.15, even when it answered UNBOUNDED:

A method that needs no ray: give every variable that has no upper bound a large one on a copy, solve, and list the variables that end at the cap. The capped model cannot be unbounded, and the variables at the cap are the ones the objective was pushing without limit:

Every carry variable runs away, which points straight at the cash rows: nothing ever takes carried money out of the year it leaves. Adding $+ r_y$ to the left side of each cash row fixes it. The cap ($10^7$ by default) has to be far above any real value of the variables. Pick it below the point where the solver's tolerances start to matter (section 10). Here $10^7$ against amounts in thousands of dollars is safe.

This is a diagnosis, not a repair: the right fix is the missing term, never a cap left in the model. A cap would turn "unbounded" into an "optimal" plan that carries $10 billion.

Problem statement

A hospital's supply team keeps a purchasing model in MathOpt: order quantities, storage limits, supplier rebates and credits, some of them continuous with no upper bound. After a refactor the model came back UNBOUNDED in production, and the team wants a check that runs before the solve and names the variables that could be to blame.

A variable can make a feasible model unbounded on its own when three things hold together:

  1. the objective rewards moving it one way (up for a positive coefficient in a maximization, down for a negative one; the reverse in a minimization);
  2. it has no bound on that side (upper_bound or lower_bound is infinite);
  3. no row stops it going that way. A row $\ell \le \sum_j a_j x_j \le u$ stops $x_j$ from growing when $a_j > 0$ and $u$ is finite, or $a_j < 0$ and $\ell$ is finite; it stops $x_j$ from falling in the two mirror cases.

Write free_to_grow(model) returning the sorted names of every variable for which all three hold. Useful calls: model.objective.is_maximize, model.objective.get_linear_coefficient(v), model.variables(), model.linear_constraints(), and for a row row.terms() (each term has .variable and .coefficient) and row.lower_bound, row.upper_bound (infinite when absent).

This check is a cheap first look: a variable it names makes any feasible model unbounded, but a model can be unbounded along a direction that moves several variables at once, which it does not see. The tests build small models and compare the names. About twenty lines.

Write next_step(result, max_gap). Summary


  • A termination reason is a claim about the model, not about your plan. INFEASIBLE means no plan exists. UNBOUNDED means plans exist and improve forever, almost always a modeling bug. INFEASIBLE_OR_UNBOUNDED means the solver could not tell which.
  • Five solvers give three answers for the same unbounded model. Every solver called the infeasible models INFEASIBLE. GLOP, PDLP, CP-SAT and HiGHS (on the MIP) hedged on the unbounded one. Presolve off settled it for GLOP and for HiGHS, and not for the others.
  • Gurobi says INF_OR_UNBD until dual reductions are off. DualReductions=0 gave definite answers, except an unbounded MIP reported OPTIMAL with an infinite objective.
  • A zero-objective copy settles INFEASIBLE_OR_UNBOUNDED on any solver: a plan means unbounded, none means infeasible.
  • Capping every free variable names the ones that run away, without needing a ray from the solver.
  • A limit without a plan means "unknown", never "infeasible".

Design Note: Ask Again Rather Than Guess


separate could have read primal_status and dual_status and inferred an answer. When the solver hedges, though, there is nothing to infer from. HiGHS's hedged answer on the carry bug claims nothing about either problem:



A second, simpler question (a zero-objective copy) costs one more solve and gives an answer you can defend. The same habit runs through this module. The pre-solve checks, the filters, the elastic model and the assumptions each change the question until the solver's answer means one thing.
