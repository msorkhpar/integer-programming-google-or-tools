# 14.1.5 Weather and Access Windows: Calendars and Optional Work

The risk-ordered schedule of the lesson *Risk grows while a circuit waits: the objective changes* went to the field. Three answers came back the same day,
from three people:

> **Crew supervisor:** The River circuits run through bottom land. Until the ground dries, around week
> 8, we can't get bucket trucks in. And K1 is at training in weeks 10 and 11; K2 is held back for storm
> duty in weeks 14 to 17.
>
> **Environmental lead:** About half of the Hills circuits cross nesting habitat. No work there from
> week 6 to week 13.
>
> **Compliance:** C05 and C16 are overdue on their trim cycle. They are not optional this year.

Each answer is a **calendar**: weeks when a circuit may be worked (an **access window**), weeks when a
crew cannot work, and circuits that must be trimmed whatever their risk. Every other circuit stays
**optional work**: trimmed if it is worth its place, otherwise left for next season at the risk the lesson
*Risk grows while a circuit waits: the objective changes* prices. (GridCap and its seasons are fictional, generated by the project's seeded generator.)
This lesson takes the project from `steps/04` to `steps/05`.

The supervisor speaks of the small season, which has two crews. The generator writes the crews'
calendar for any number of crews: K1 is off for training in weeks 10 and 11, and the season's *last*
crew, when there is more than one, is held back for storm duty in weeks 14 to 17. That is K2 in the
small season and K6 in the medium one; the tiny season's single crew has only the training weeks.

## The Plan Ignores Every Calendar, Because the Model Has No Word for One

`steps/04` knows a season as one stretch of 24 weeks in which every crew works every week and any
circuit may be left. `calendar_plan.py` reads `steps/04`'s small-season plan with a checker written
from the three answers above (it shares no code with the model, lesson 4.5.4's practice) and lists
what the plan breaks:

```text
calendars: K1 off weeks 10-11, K2 off weeks 14-17
must: C05 C16 | windows: 12 of 20 circuits
steps/04 plan (FEASIBLE): 7 calendar rules broken
  C01 weeks 0-4 are outside its windows [[8, 23]]
  C04 weeks 5-11 are outside its windows [[8, 23]]
  C04 weeks 5-11 on K1, who is off weeks 10-11
  C09 weeks 7-10 are outside its windows [[8, 23]]
  C12 weeks 11-17 are outside its windows [[0, 5], [14, 23]]
  C12 weeks 11-17 on K2, who is off weeks 14-17
  C16 is a must and is not trimmed
```

Seven broken rules in nine trims, and a compliance circuit left out. None of it is a bug in the sense
of wrong code: the model was never told. The `steps/04` plan above was solved with repeatable
settings, as in the lesson *Risk grows while a circuit waits: the objective changes*, so the list repeats from run to run.

## Each Calendar Is a Constraint Kind CP-SAT Already Has

Three rules, and for each the design question is which CP-SAT construct states it. Lesson 4.5.2 met
two of them on GridCap's outage windows; the options:

| Rule | Chosen | Alternative, and why not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A trim lies inside one access window | the start's **domain** is the union of the starts that fit a window: $`s \in \bigcup_j [a_j,\, b_j - w + 1]`$ | a Boolean per window and enforced rows `a_j <= s` and `s + w - 1 <= b_j`: more variables to say what a domain says for free |
| A crew is off some weeks | a **fixed interval** for each span off, added to that crew's NoOverlap | one start bound per trim and span: a pair of rows per trim, crew and span, and wrong if a trim may fit before the span |
| A must is trimmed | $`t_c = 1`$ | forcing the presence of one crew option: but which crew? $`t_c`$ already says "some crew" |

The window and the time off are the same idea from two sides. A window shrinks *where a trim may
start*; time off occupies *the crew's line* so nothing else may sit there. Both are data the model
reads, not code paths, and both are invisible to every other row.

What about a window too short for its trim, like a 7-week trim in a 6-week window? Then that window
contributes no starts, and if no window fits, the start domain is empty. Such a circuit gets no crew
options at all and waits for next season, exactly like a trim longer than the season in the lesson
*A trim takes weeks on one crew: from MILP to CP-SAT intervals*. And a must circuit that cannot be scheduled makes the whole season **infeasible**: no plan
keeps every rule. That is the right answer, and the lesson *Planner overrides and why a plan failed* is about saying *why*.

## The Model

The lesson *Risk grows while a circuit waits: the objective changes*'s model with three changes, in full:

**Sets.** $`C`$, circuits; $`K`$, crews; weeks $`0, \dots, H - 1`$; $`M \subseteq C`$, the must
circuits ← *this lesson*; for each circuit $`c`$, its windows $`W_c`$, each a span $`[a, b]`$ of weeks
(no windows means $`[0, H - 1]`$) ← *this lesson*; for each crew $`k`$, its spans off $`O_k`$
← *this lesson*.

**Parameters.** $`\text{cost}_c`$, $`B`$ ($k); $`w_c`$ (weeks); $`R_c(e)`$ ($k); $`D = 26`$.

**Variables.** $`t_c \in \{0,1\}`$; $`p_{c,k} \in \{0,1\}`$;
$`s_{c,k} \in S_c = \bigcup_{[a,b] \in W_c,\ b - w_c + 1 \ge a} \{a, \dots, b - w_c + 1\}`$
← *this lesson: the window is the start's domain*; the optional interval
$`I_{c,k} = [s_{c,k}, s_{c,k} + w_c)`$; $`e_c \in \{w_c..H\} \cup \{H + D\}`$; $`\rho_c`$. A circuit with
$`S_c = \emptyset`$ has no $`p, s, I`$, so $`t_c = 0`$.

**Objective.**

```math
\min \; \sum_{c \in C} \rho_c
```

**Constraints.**

```math
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k \in K} p_{c,k} &= t_c && \forall c \in C && \text{(once}_c) \\
p_{c,k} = 1 \;&\Rightarrow\; e_c = s_{c,k} + w_c && \forall c, k && \text{(ends}_{c,k}) \\
t_c = 0 \;&\Rightarrow\; e_c = H + D && \forall c \in C && \text{(waits}_c) \\
\rho_c &= R_c[e_c] && \forall c \in C && \text{(risk}_c) \\
t_c &= 1 && \forall c \in M && \text{(must}_c) \quad \leftarrow \text{this lesson} \\
\text{NoOverlap}\big(\{ I_{c,k} : c \in C \} \cup \{ [a, b+1) : [a,b] \in O_k \}\big) & && \forall k \in K && \text{(crew}_k) \quad \leftarrow \text{now with time off} \\
\sum_{c \in C} \text{cost}_c\, t_c &\le B && && \text{(budget)}
\end{aligned}
```

The window rule (window$`_c`$ in the code's comments) is not a row: it is the domain $`S_c`$.

Spans are inclusive at both ends, as people say them ("weeks 10 and 11"), and converted to CP-SAT's
half-open intervals in exactly one place, the model. The loader reads the new columns with defaults,
and its docstring says what a missing value means:

<!-- snapshot: steps/05/vegcrew/data.py -->
```python
    """Read the columns the planner uses. Columns it does not use yet are ignored.

    A circuit with no windows may be trimmed in any week, one with no must flag is optional work,
    and a season with no crew_off has every crew at work every week.
    """
    circuits = tuple(Circuit(c["id"], c["region"], int(c["miles"]), int(c["cost"]), int(c["risk"]),
                             int(c["weeks"]), float(c["growth"]), spans(c.get("windows") or []),
                             bool(c.get("must", False)))
                     for c in raw["circuits"])
    crew_off = {k: spans(weeks) for k, weeks in (raw.get("crew_off") or {}).items()}

```

Defaults are a decision, not a convenience: "no windows" must mean the whole season, never "no
weeks", or every season exported before the column existed would plan nothing.

**`vegcrew/planner.py`.** A new function, `start_domain(c, h)`, turns a circuit's windows into its
start domain. It is the window rule, and the page leaves its code to the practice. It returns
$`S_c`$ of *The Model* above as a `cp_model.Domain`, for a season of `h` weeks: every first week that
keeps a trim of $`w_c`$ weeks inside one of the circuit's windows and inside the season. Each window
$`[a, b]`$ (inclusive at both ends; no windows means the one window $`[0, h - 1]`$) gives the starts
$`a, \dots, b - w_c + 1`$, with $`b`$ first cut back to $`h - 1`$ if it runs past the season, and a
window too short for the trim, where $`b - w_c + 1 < a`$, gives none. `Domain.from_intervals`, the
call the end week of the lesson *Risk grows while a circuit waits: the objective changes* uses, takes the list of `[first, last]` pairs (each inclusive), and an
empty list gives an empty domain, which `is_empty()` reports.

In `build`, the start of each crew option takes that domain, and "too long for the season" becomes the
more general "no window fits":

## Every Calendar Kept, at a Price

`calendar_plan.py` plans the small season with `steps/05` (with `WORKERS` set to 1; the plan is proved
optimal well inside the time limit, so it repeats) and runs the same checker:

```text
season small-54: 20 circuits, budget $962k, crews 2, weeks 24
status OPTIMAL: 8 trims, cost $945k, risk carried $4,571k
  K1 7777777222xx44444446666.  C17:0-6 C02:7-9 C04:12-18 C06:19-22
  K2 33666666611111xxxx5555..  C03:0-1 C16:2-8 C01:9-13 C05:18-21
steps/05 plan: 0 calendar rules broken
```

Every rule holds. K1 is idle for its training weeks and starts C04 right after; K2 does C01 before its
storm duty and the must C05 after it. C16 (a 7-week must) runs early on K2, and every River circuit
starts at week 8 or later.

The calendars cost risk. The `steps/04` plan carried $3,989k without them, and it was a real plan
of that model, so the best plan without calendars carries at most $3,989k; the best plan with them
carries $4,571k (OPTIMAL). The calendars cost at least $582k of risk over the season, and the plan
drops from nine trims to eight. That number is worth showing the people who set the calendars: it
is what the training weeks, the nesting closure and the wet ground cost in risk.

The calendars also made the model *easier*: the small season, which `steps/04` could not prove in 20
units of deterministic time, is proved optimal here. Narrow domains and blocked weeks leave fewer
orders to try.

The others: a trim inside a single window; a window shorter than the trim, which leaves the circuit

exact week where the rule decides one. Since risk grows, the earliest allowed week is always the best,
so "starts at 5" is the rule's answer, not one of several.

the longest function in the project, and the next lesson's overrides will push it to be split.

## Summary

- **The plan ignored every calendar, because the model had no word for one.** A checker written from
  the rules found seven broken rules in the `steps/04` plan.
- **Each calendar is a constraint kind CP-SAT already has.** A window is the start's domain, time off
  is a fixed interval in the crew's NoOverlap, a must is $`t_c = 1`$; all other work stays optional.
- **Every calendar kept, at a price:** OPTIMAL at $4,571k, at least $582k more than without
  calendars, and one trim fewer.
- **Seven tests, one per calendar case,** each with a single right week.

## Reference

| Rule | Formulation | CP-SAT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| window | $`s_{c,k} \in S_c`$ | `new_int_var_from_domain(start_domain(c, h), ...)` |

| time off | fixed $`[a, b+1)`$ in crew$`_k`$ | `new_fixed_size_interval_var(first, last - first + 1, ...)` |
| must | $`t_c = 1`$ | `add(t == 1)` |

## Design Note: A Window Belongs to the Data, a Domain to the Model

The inventory says "weeks 8 to 23", inclusive, because that is how people speak. CP-SAT wants the
set of allowed starts for a trim of a given length. The translation happens in one function,
`start_domain`, which knows about trim lengths and the season's end and nothing else. Every other
part of the code (the generator, the loader, the report, the checker in the tests) speaks in the
people's terms. Keep such translations in one place, small and named, and an off-by-one in a window
is a one-line fix with one test to prove it.
