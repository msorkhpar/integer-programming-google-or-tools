# 14.1.3 A Trim Takes Weeks on One Crew: From MILP to CP-SAT Intervals

GridCap's crew supervisor booked the month plan of the lesson *Crews and months: capacity over time* and came back within a day:

> Month 1 says C12, 7 crew-weeks, out of 8. That means both my crews on C12 for three and a half
> weeks each. I don't split a circuit between crews: the crew that starts a circuit finishes it, one
> week after another, and it hands nothing over. And C12 takes one crew seven weeks, so it can't sit
> inside one month anyway. Give me a crew and a start week for every trim.

Two facts break the month model at once: a trim belongs to **one crew**, and it occupies a run of
**consecutive weeks** that can cross a month boundary. This lesson takes the project from `steps/02`
to `steps/03` and, on the way, from a MILP solved by HiGHS to a CP-SAT model built from interval
variables. (GridCap and its seasons are fictional, generated by the project's seeded generator.)

## The Month Model Promises Work One Crew Cannot Do, and Refuses Work One Crew Can

The month model treats a month's crew-weeks as one pool. `month_flaw.py` asks both models about two
hand-made seasons with a single 6-week trim, and lists every trim of the small season's month plan
that only fits by splitting it across crews:

```text
month model     a 6-week trim, 1 crew, 2 months: not trimmed
month model     a 6-week trim, 2 crews, 1 month: trimmed in month 1
interval model  a 6-week trim, 1 crew, 2 months: trimmed by K1 in weeks 0-5
interval model  a 6-week trim, 2 crews, 1 month: not trimmed
month model on the small season, 2 crews, 4-week months:
  C12 needs 7 crew-weeks in month 1: only by splitting it across 2 crews
  C17 needs 7 crew-weeks in month 2: only by splitting it across 2 crews
  C04 needs 7 crew-weeks in month 3: only by splitting it across 2 crews
  C13 needs 5 crew-weeks in month 5: only by splitting it across 2 crews
  C01 needs 5 crew-weeks in month 6: only by splitting it across 2 crews
```

The model is wrong in both directions. With one crew and two months it refuses the trim, because no
single month holds 6 crew-weeks, although one crew working weeks 0 to 5 does it easily. With two crews
and one month it accepts the trim, because 6 is less than the month's 8, although no one crew can
work 6 weeks in a 4-week month. On the small season, five of the nine trims are booked in a way the
supervisor has just said is impossible. A model that is wrong in both directions cannot be fixed by
tightening one row: the unit of the model, a month of pooled crew-weeks, is the wrong unit.

## A Trim Is an Optional Interval on Each Crew, and a Crew's Intervals Never Overlap

What the supervisor described is a scheduling problem: jobs (trims) with fixed durations, machines
(crews) that do one job at a time, and a choice of machine per job. Lesson 4.5.1 built exactly this
with CP-SAT interval variables. The design options:

| Option | What it gives | Why not, or why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep the MILP, with one binary per circuit, crew and start week, and a row per crew and week | exact, and still HiGHS | $`20 \times 2 \times 24 = 960`$ binaries on the small season and a row per crew-week; it grows with the horizon (lesson 4.5.1's design note weighs intervals against week-by-week Booleans) |
| A MILP with integer start times and big-M "either before or after" rows per pair of trims on a crew | few variables | pairwise big-M rows with weak LP bounds (lesson 6.2.4) |
| CP-SAT: per circuit and crew, a Boolean "on this crew", a start week, and an optional interval tied to both; NoOverlap per crew | the supervisor's words, one constraint per word; the size does not grow with the number of weeks | chosen: CP-SAT reasons about intervals directly (lessons 4.5.1 and 4.4.4) |

An **interval variable** ties a start, a size and an end together (lesson 4.5.1). An **optional
interval** also has a presence literal: when the literal is false the interval is "simply ignored by
all constraints", in the words of the `new_optional_fixed_size_interval_var` docstring in the pinned
OR-Tools. So each circuit gets one optional interval per crew, at most one of them is present, and
**NoOverlap** on a crew's intervals (which, its docstring says, keeps all *present* intervals from
overlapping in time) is the whole of "one trim at a time".

Changing solvers is a big step, and the requirement justifies it: the rows that remain (once, budget)
are as easy in CP-SAT as in MathOpt, while the new rule is native to CP-SAT and awkward in a MILP.
CP-SAT needs integer data, which this project already has: weeks, $k and crew counts are whole numbers.

## The Model

The month model of the lesson *Crews and months: capacity over time*, before:

```math
\max \sum_{c} \text{risk}_c \sum_{m} x_{c,m} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad
\sum_m x_{c,m} \le 1, \;\; \sum_c w_c x_{c,m} \le 4K, \;\; \sum_c \text{cost}_c \sum_m x_{c,m} \le B,
\;\; x_{c,m} \in \{0,1\}
```

After, in full:

**Sets.** $`C`$, the circuits; $`K`$, the crews $`\{K1, \dots\}`$; weeks $`0, \dots, H - 1`$ with
$`H = 4 \times \text{months} = 24`$. ← *this lesson: crews are named, time is in weeks*

**Parameters.** $`\text{cost}_c`$ ($k); $`\text{risk}_c`$ ($k a year); $`B`$ ($k); $`w_c`$, the weeks
one crew takes to trim $`c`$. Only circuits with $`w_c \le H`$ get variables.

**Variables.** For every $`c \in C`$ and $`k \in K`$:
$`p_{c,k} \in \{0, 1\}`$, 1 if crew $`k`$ trims $`c`$; $`s_{c,k} \in \{0, \dots, H - w_c\}`$, its first
week; and the optional interval $`I_{c,k} = [s_{c,k},\, s_{c,k} + w_c)`$, present exactly when
$`p_{c,k} = 1`$. ← *this lesson: replaces $`x_{c,m}`$*

**Objective.**

```math
\max \; \sum_{c \in C} \text{risk}_c \sum_{k \in K} p_{c,k}
```

**Constraints.**

```math
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k \in K} p_{c,k} &\le 1 && \forall c \in C && \text{(once}_c) \\
\text{NoOverlap}\big(\{ I_{c,k} : c \in C \}\big) & && \forall k \in K && \text{(crew}_k) \quad \leftarrow \text{this lesson} \\
\sum_{c \in C} \text{cost}_c \sum_{k \in K} p_{c,k} &\le B && && \text{(budget)}
\end{aligned}
```

The crews$`_m`$ rows are gone: nothing pools crew-weeks any more. The season's end is now a bound on
each start, $`s_{c,k} \le H - w_c`$, so every trim finishes inside the season. CP-SAT rows have no
names here, so the code marks each with its label in a comment.

## The Change, File by File

**`vegcrew/planner.py`** is rewritten; it is the model. `Trim` replaces `month` with a crew, a
first week and an end week (the week after the last, so `end - start` is the trim's length):

<!-- snapshot: steps/03/vegcrew/planner.py -->
```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Trim:
    cid: str
    crew: str                                       # K1, K2, ...
    start: int                                      # first week, counted from 0
    end: int                                        # the week after the last: end - start = weeks
```

The MathOpt imports, `EXACT`, `STATUS` and `solve_quietly` go: CP-SAT has its own status names and
prints nothing unless asked. `crew_ids` and `horizon`, stubs in `steps/02`, now return the crews'
names and the season's length in weeks. The model's variables live in a small mutable dataclass, so
`make_plan` (and later lessons) can read them by name:

<!-- snapshot: steps/03/vegcrew/planner.py -->
```python
@dataclass
class TrimModel:
    model: cp_model.CpModel
    present: dict[tuple[str, str], cp_model.IntVar] = field(default_factory=dict)    # (circuit, crew)
    start: dict[tuple[str, str], cp_model.IntVar] = field(default_factory=dict)
    work: dict[tuple[str, str], cp_model.IntervalVar] = field(default_factory=dict)

    def trimmed(self, cid: str) -> cp_model.LinearExprT:
        return sum(p for (c, _), p in self.present.items() if c == cid)
```

`build`, before a MathOpt model over months, is now the CP-SAT model, one line per part of the
formulation. It starts with an empty `TrimModel` and the season's length in weeks:

<!-- snapshot: steps/03/vegcrew/planner.py -->
```python
def build(season: Season) -> TrimModel:
    tm = TrimModel(cp_model.CpModel())
    h = horizon(season)
```

The middle of `build` is the new model, and the page leaves it to the practice. It is the variables
and the first two constraint families of *The Model* above:

- for each circuit in the season's order with $`w_c \le H`$, and for each crew from K1 on: the
  presence literal $`p_{c,k}`$ in `tm.present` (named `p_<cid>_<crew>`), the start $`s_{c,k}`$ in
  `tm.start`, an integer from 0 to $`H - w_c`$ (named `s_<cid>_<crew>`), and the optional interval
  $`I_{c,k}`$ in `tm.work`, of fixed size $`w_c`$, present when $`p_{c,k}`$ is (named
  `w_<cid>_<crew>`); then that circuit's (once$`_c`$), at most one of its presence literals true;
- after every circuit, for each crew from K1 on, (crew$`_k`$): no two of that crew's intervals overlap.

All three dictionaries are keyed by (circuit id, crew id). Lesson 4.5.1 has the calls:
`new_bool_var`, `new_int_var`, `new_optional_fixed_size_interval_var`, `add_at_most_one` and
`add_no_overlap`. The objective does not say in which order the work is done, so another build order
can give another plan that is just as good; the tests accept it, but the practice's expected report
comes from the order above. The end of `build` is the two parts the month model already had, the
(budget) row and the objective, each over `tm.trimmed`:

<!-- snapshot: steps/03/vegcrew/planner.py -->
```python
    tm.model.add(sum(c.cost * tm.trimmed(c.cid) for c in season.circuits) <= season.budget)   # budget
    tm.model.maximize(sum(c.risk * tm.trimmed(c.cid) for c in season.circuits))
    return tm
```

A circuit longer than the season gets no variables at all, so it cannot be chosen: the same idea as
a timing window enforced by the variables you never create (lesson 6.4.1).

`make_plan` sets two parameters and reads each present interval back as a trim:

<!-- snapshot: steps/03/vegcrew/planner.py -->
```python
    tm = build(season)
    solver = cp_model.CpSolver()
    solver.parameters.max_time_in_seconds = TIME_LIMIT
    solver.parameters.num_workers = WORKERS
    status = solver.status_name(solver.solve(tm.model))
    if status not in ("OPTIMAL", "FEASIBLE"):
        return Plan(status)
    trims = {}
    for (cid, k), p in tm.present.items():
        if solver.boolean_value(p):
            start = solver.value(tm.start[cid, k])
            trims[cid] = Trim(cid, k, start, start + season.circuit(cid).weeks)
```

`TIME_LIMIT = 10.0` seconds and `WORKERS = 4` parallel search workers (lesson 4.6.1): a stop for a
hard season and some parallel search. Both choices have a cost that the lesson *The same plan every run: determinism and regression tests* makes visible.
`main.py`, which prints the tiny season's report, sets `planner.WORKERS = 1` before it plans, for the
reason given with `crew_plan.py` below.

**`vegcrew/report.py`.** One line per crew, one character per week: the circuit's last digit while
it is worked, a dot when the crew is free. The supervisor reads a crew's season in one line.

**`vegcrew/data.py` and `generate.py`** do not change: `weeks` and `crews` from the lesson *Crews and months: capacity over time* already
say everything the new model needs. The data was right; only its use was wrong.

## Every Trim Has a Crew and a Run of Weeks

`crew_plan.py` plans the small and tiny seasons with `steps/03`. (It sets the planner's `WORKERS` to
1 first, so the printed plan repeats from run to run; why four workers might not is the lesson *The same plan every run: determinism and regression tests*.)

```text
season small-54: 20 circuits, budget $962k, crews 2, weeks 24
status OPTIMAL: 9 trims, cost $952k, risk removed $2,114k a year
  K1 333334444444111112222222  C13:0-4 C04:5-11 C01:12-16 C12:17-23
  K2 006666999977777775555...  C10:0-1 C06:2-5 C09:6-9 C17:10-16 C05:17-20
season tiny-54: 6 circuits, budget $170k, crews 1, weeks 24
status OPTIMAL: 3 trims, cost $148k, risk removed $355k a year
  K1 5555111333..............  C05:0-3 C01:4-6 C03:7-9
small model: 80 variables, 63 constraints (40 optional intervals)
```

The objective counts risk removed and nothing else, so on the tiny season any order of the three
trims, with or without idle weeks between them, is as good as the one printed. Which one CP-SAT
returns depends on the model as built, even with one worker. `crew_plan.py` builds the same model
once more from the circuits in reverse order:

```text
tiny, the same model built from the circuits in reverse order:
  K1 333.1115555.............  C03:0-2 C01:4-6 C05:7-10
```

Another order, and an idle week 3. It is as correct a plan as the first, and keeps every rule; only
the report differs. That is why this lesson's practice states the build order and the single
worker its expected report comes from.

C12 runs on K1 for weeks 17 to 23, seven weeks straight; C04 crosses the month boundary at week 8.
The plan is again the knapsack's nine circuits and its $2,114k a year: on this season the money binds
and the crews, scheduled week by week, can do all of it. The month model's $5k loss in the lesson *Crews and months: capacity over time*
was an artifact of pooling crews into months, not a real limit.

The model is also smaller than the month model's 120 binaries: 40 presence Booleans and 40 start
weeks, one pair per circuit and crew, whatever the number of weeks. Its 63 constraints are the 40
intervals (CP-SAT counts each as a constraint), 20 once rows, 2 NoOverlaps and the budget.

The biggest change of the module so far left the code smaller. Interval variables say in one call what
the MILP needed a family of rows for, and the HiGHS plumbing went with HiGHS. The style changed in two
ways. Row names (`once_C07`) became comments, because CP-SAT constraints are not looked up by name in
this code. And the model's variables moved into `TrimModel`, a mutable dataclass, where `steps/02`
returned a dict: the model now has three kinds of variable, and later lessons add more.

## Summary

- **The month model is wrong in both directions.** It refuses a 6-week trim one crew can do across
  two months, and accepts one that two crews could only do by splitting it.
- **A trim is an optional interval on each crew, and a crew's intervals never overlap.** A presence
  Boolean, a start week and `new_optional_fixed_size_interval_var` per circuit and crew;
  `add_at_most_one` per circuit; `add_no_overlap` per crew.
- **Every trim has a crew and a run of weeks.** The small season gets the knapsack's nine circuits
  back, scheduled on two crews.
- **Five new tests;** the month tests retire with the months, and their rule lives on.

## Design Note: Change the Solver When the Rule Changes Its Kind

The project changed solvers here, and only here. The knapsack and the month rows were linear
inequalities over yes-or-no choices, the home ground of a MILP. "One crew, consecutive weeks, one job
at a time" is a sequencing rule, and the honest MILP forms of it are either large (a binary per week)
or weak (big-M pairs). When a requirement changes the *kind* of rule, it is worth asking whether the
solver still fits; when it adds another rule of the same kind, it is not. Keeping `Plan` and `Trim`
as the boundary made the switch cost one file.
