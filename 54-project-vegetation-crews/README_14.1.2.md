# 14.1.2 Crews and Months: Capacity Over Time

The first plan (the lesson *The first request: which circuits to trim this year*) went to GridCap's vegetation program manager, who sent it to the
crew supervisor. The supervisor's answer is the second requirement:

> Your list needs 45 crew-weeks of trimming. I have two crews from April to September, and I plan
> them a month at a time: each crew gives me four weeks a month. Tell me which circuits to trim *and
> in which month*, without ever booking more work in a month than my crews can do.

A **crew-week** is one crew working for one week. Each circuit now carries the crew-weeks its trim
takes, a new column in the inventory. (GridCap and its seasons are fictional, generated by the
project's own seeded generator.) This lesson takes the project from `steps/01` to `steps/02`.

## The Knapsack Cannot See Time or Crews

`steps/01` answers "which", never "when". Its one row is money; nothing in it knows a crew exists, so
it will happily fund three long trims for a month that has eight crew-weeks in it. And the plan has no
field to say *when* a trim happens, so even a correct schedule could not be reported.

Is the knapsack's plan feasible for the crews anyway? Its circuits need 45 crew-weeks and the season
has 48, so the totals suggest yes. `months_plan.py` prints both numbers:

```text
the first plan needs 45 crew-weeks; the season has 48 (2 crews x 6 months x 4 weeks)
```

But the supervisor books months, not seasons. A month has 8 crew-weeks, and three of the first plan's
circuits need 7 each (C04, C12, C17), which leaves 1 crew-week in each of their months for the six
other circuits, none of which is that short. Fitting work into months is a packing problem, and the
totals cannot answer it. A model has to.

## Capacity Over Time Is One Capacity Row per Month, Over Start-Month Binaries

The design decision is how to represent time. Three options:

| Option | What it gives | Why not, or why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep $`x_c`$ and add one season-long crew row, $`\sum_c w_c x_c \le 48`$ | the smallest change | the 45 against 48 check above: it passes plans that no month can hold |
| One binary per circuit and week, with the trim's weeks laid out week by week | exact timing | 24 times the variables and a row per week; the supervisor asked for months |
| One binary per circuit and month, $`x_{c,m}`$, and a capacity row per month | the supervisor's own unit of planning | chosen: the time-indexed pattern of lesson 6.4.1, with months as the periods |

$`x_{c,m} = 1`$ means circuit $`c`$ is trimmed in month $`m`$. Every rule of the first model survives
by replacing $`x_c`$ with $`\sum_m x_{c,m}`$, which is 1 if the circuit is trimmed at all (lesson
6.4.1 calls that sum `started`; here it is `trimmed`). A trim is assumed to fit in the month it is
booked in, by as many crews as that takes; the lesson *A trim takes weeks on one crew: from MILP to CP-SAT intervals* is about what that assumption hides.

## The Model

**Sets.** $`C`$, the circuits; $`M = \{1, \dots, 6\}`$, the months of the season. ← *this lesson: M*

**Parameters.** $`\text{cost}_c`$ ($k); $`\text{risk}_c`$ ($k a year); $`B`$, the budget ($k);
$`w_c`$, the crew-weeks to trim $`c`$; $`K`$, the number of crews; 4, the weeks in a month.
← *this lesson: $`w_c`$, $`K`$*

**Variables.** $`x_{c,m} \in \{0, 1\}`$ for every $`c \in C`$, $`m \in M`$: 1 if circuit $`c`$ is
trimmed in month $`m`$. ← *this lesson: replaces $`x_c`$*

**Objective.**

```math
\max \; \sum_{c \in C} \text{risk}_c \sum_{m \in M} x_{c,m}
```

**Constraints.**

```math
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{m \in M} x_{c,m} &\le 1 && \forall c \in C && \text{(once}_c) \quad \leftarrow \text{this lesson} \\
\sum_{c \in C} w_c \, x_{c,m} &\le 4K && \forall m \in M && \text{(crews}_m) \quad \leftarrow \text{this lesson} \\
\sum_{c \in C} \text{cost}_c \sum_{m \in M} x_{c,m} &\le B && && \text{(budget)}
\end{aligned}
```

| Label | Row name in `planner.py` |
| --- | --- |
| once$`_c`$ | `once_C07` |
| crews$`_m`$ | `crews_3` |
| budget | `budget` |

Before this lesson the model was 1.1.1's knapsack: variables $`x_c`$, the objective
$`\max \sum_c \text{risk}_c x_c`$ and the budget row. The once rows are new because there are now
several ways to trim one circuit, and without them the model could book C17 in two months and count
its risk twice (lesson 6.4.1's trap).

`WEEKS_PER_MONTH = 4` lives here too, as a fact about the data, so that the model and the report use
the same number.

**`vegcrew/planner.py`.** Four changes. `Trim` gains `month`, the only new thing a plan says.
`build` is rewritten: the variables are keyed by circuit and month, a small `trimmed` function names
the sum that replaces $`x_c`$, and each row carries its label. The variables and `trimmed` come
first:

<!-- snapshot: steps/02/vegcrew/planner.py -->
```python
def build(season: Season) -> tuple[mathopt.Model, dict[tuple[str, int], mathopt.Variable]]:
    model = mathopt.Model(name=f"trim_{season.name}")
    months = range(1, season.months + 1)
    x = {(c.cid, m): model.add_binary_variable(name=f"x_{c.cid}_{m}") for c in season.circuits for m in months}

    def trimmed(cid: str) -> mathopt.LinearExpression:
        return mathopt.fast_sum(x[cid, m] for m in months)
```

The rows and the objective are the rest of `build`, and the page leaves them to the practice. They
are the constraints of *The Model* above, each added with `add_linear_constraint` under the row name
in its table:

- (once$`_c`$), `once_<cid>`, one per circuit: `trimmed(cid)` at most 1;
- (crews$`_m`$), `crews_<m>`, one per month: the summed $`w_c \, x_{c,m}`$ over the circuits, that
  month's crew-weeks, at most `season.crews * WEEKS_PER_MONTH`;
- (budget), `budget`: the summed $`\text{cost}_c`$ times `trimmed(cid)` at most the season's budget;
- the objective: maximize the summed $`\text{risk}_c`$ times `trimmed(cid)`.

Then `build` returns the model and `x`. Add them in that order: the once rows in the season's circuit
order, the crews rows from month 1 to the last, the budget row, the objective. The objective does not
name the month, so another order can give other months that are just as good (*The Crews Change the
Plan: Two Circuits Out, Two In* below builds the same model in two orders); the tests accept them, but the practice's expected
report comes from this order.

`sum` became `mathopt.fast_sum`. Its docstring in the pinned OR-Tools explains why that is more than
speed: on an empty input, `sum` returns the integer 0, and `0 <= 8` is a Python `True`, which
`add_linear_constraint` refuses; `fast_sum` returns an empty linear expression, so a month row over a
season with no circuits still builds. With one row per month, an empty sum is no longer far-fetched.

`make_plan` reads the month off the variables that are 1:

<!-- snapshot: steps/02/vegcrew/planner.py -->
```python
    trims = {cid: Trim(cid, m) for (cid, m), v in x.items() if result.variable_values(v) > 0.5}
```

The fourth change was forced by the solver, not the requirement. The month model is the first in
this project on which the HiGHS bundled with OR-Tools 9.15 prints a debug line nobody asked for.
`months_plan.py` solves the model in a child process without protection and
shows what reached its standard output:

```text
without solve_quietly the program printed 5 lines, nothing asked for:
  HighsMipSolverData::transformNewIntegerFeasibleSolution tmpSolver.run();
```

In a script that prints a report, or a service that returns JSON on standard output, those lines are
corruption. `solve_quietly` points standard output at `/dev/null` for the solve and flushes the C
buffer before restoring it, the same guard as `solve_highs` in the `timing_model.py` of lesson 6.4.1:

<!-- snapshot: steps/02/vegcrew/planner.py -->
```python
    sys.stdout.flush()
    saved, sink = os.dup(1), os.open(os.devnull, os.O_WRONLY)
    os.dup2(sink, 1)
    try:
        return mathopt.solve(model, mathopt.SolverType.HIGHS, params=EXACT)
    finally:
        ctypes.CDLL(None).fflush(None)
        os.dup2(saved, 1)
        os.close(saved)
        os.close(sink)
```

**`vegcrew/report.py`.** One line per month instead of one per circuit, with the crew-weeks used
against the month's capacity, because that is what the supervisor reads.

## The Crews Change the Plan: Two Circuits Out, Two In

`months_plan.py` plans the small season with `steps/02` and sets it beside the first plan:

```text
season small-54: 20 circuits, budget $962k, crews 2, months 6
status OPTIMAL: 9 trims, cost $958k, risk removed $2,109k a year
  month 1:  7/8 crew-weeks  C12(7)
  month 2:  7/8 crew-weeks  C17(7)
  month 3:  7/8 crew-weeks  C04(7)
  month 4:  8/8 crew-weeks  C09(4) C20(4)
  month 5:  8/8 crew-weeks  C02(3) C13(5)
  month 6:  7/8 crew-weeks  C01(5) C10(2)
first plan (lesson 14.1.1): 9 trims, risk removed $2,114k a year
dropped: C05 C06  added: C02 C20
the first plan needs 45 crew-weeks; the season has 48 (2 crews x 6 months x 4 weeks)
model: 120 binary variables, 27 rows
```

Every month is within its 8 crew-weeks, and the price of the crews is $5k a year of risk: C05 and C06
(4 crew-weeks each in this inventory) are replaced by C02 and C20, whose sizes pack better around the
three 7-week circuits. The model grew from 20 variables and 1 row to 120 variables (20 circuits times
6 months) and 27 rows (20 once rows, 6 crew rows, the budget).

Notice what the objective does not say: the month. Moving C12 from month 1 to month 6 removes the
same risk, so the solver picks months only to make the packing work, and any month that packs is as
good as any other. The lesson *Risk grows while a circuit waits: the objective changes* is where that stops being true.

Which of those equally good months HiGHS returns is decided by the model as built, not by the rules.
HiGHS returns the same plan every time it solves the same model, but the same model built in another
order is another solve. `months_plan.py` builds the tiny season's model twice, once as `build` does
(the variables circuit by circuit) and once with the variables made month by month, with the same
rows and objective:

```text
tiny season, the same model built two ways:
  variables circuit by circuit: risk removed $355k, C05 in month 2, C01 in month 4, C03 in month 6
  variables month by month:     risk removed $355k, C05 in month 2, C03 in month 4, C01 in month 5
```

Both plans are optimal and keep every rule. Only the month lines of the report differ, which is
why this lesson's practice states the build order its expected report comes from.

## Three Tests State the Crew Rule

`steps/02` adds `tests/test_months.py`. One test checks every month of the small season's plan
against its capacity, reading the crew-weeks from the inventory rows rather than from the code under
test. Two are small enough to solve by hand:

<!-- snapshot: steps/02/tests/test_months.py -->
```python
def test_crews_can_stop_a_plan_the_budget_allows():
    # Money for both, but one crew for one month does 4 crew-weeks: only one 3-week trim fits.
    s = season(1000, ("A", 10, 50, 3), ("B", 10, 40, 3), crews=1, months=1)
    plan = make_plan(s)
    assert (plan.status, sorted(plan.trims)) == ("OPTIMAL", ["A"])


def test_a_second_month_takes_the_trim_the_first_cannot():
    s = season(1000, ("A", 10, 50, 3), ("B", 10, 40, 3), crews=1, months=2)
    plan = make_plan(s)
    assert sorted(plan.trims) == ["A", "B"]
    assert {t.month for t in plan.trims.values()} == {1, 2}
```

The pair is deliberate: the same two circuits, one month refused and two months allowed. A model
with no crew rows passes neither the first nor (since it has no months) the second. The test helper
`season(...)` in `test_planner.py` also changed: it takes `crews` and `months`, and a crew-weeks
entry per circuit that defaults to 1, so the four tests of the lesson *The first request: which circuits to trim this year* still run unchanged.

## Summary

- **The knapsack cannot see time or crews.** Season totals (45 crew-weeks against 48) pass plans that
  no month can hold.
- **Capacity over time is one capacity row per month, over start-month binaries.** $`x_c`$ becomes
  $`\sum_m x_{c,m}`$ in every old rule, and a once row per circuit stops double counting.
- **The crews change the plan.** Two circuits swap out, the risk removed falls by $5k a year, and the
  objective does not care which month a trim takes.
- **Three tests state the crew rule,** with a refused and an allowed case built from the same circuits.

## Design Note: Time as Data, Not as Code

The months are a range built from `season.months`, never a literal 6 in the model. The day the
supervisor asks for a seven-month season, or for two-week periods, the data changes and the model
does not. The same goes for `WEEKS_PER_MONTH`: one constant, read by the model and the report alike,
so the plan and its printout cannot disagree about what a month holds.
